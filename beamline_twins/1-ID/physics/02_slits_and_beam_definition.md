# 2. Slits: what changes when you change a slit

Twin elements: `wbSlits` (white beam, hutch A), `slitB`, `slitCus`/`slitCds`, `slitEus`/`slitEds` (Kohzu, 4 blades each: size = opening, centre = position of the opening; see `DOMAIN.md`). Citation keys: `README.md`.

## 2.1 White-beam (WB) slits: the source aperture
They set the angular acceptance of the white beam into the mono, so they set **flux, thermal load and what the optics downstream can ever see**.
- **Typical sizes in the logs:** 0.3 x 0.3 mm (13 mentions) and 0.5 x 0.5 mm (13), 0.6 x 0.6 (6), 0.4 x 0.4 (3), with the position quoted as `[h, v]` centre in mm (e.g. [-0.02, -0.08] Jan 2025; [0.06, -0.23] then [0.04, -0.23] Jun 2025; [0.0, -0.07] Feb 2026) [S25a:L35], [S25b:L21-35], [M26a:L43]. Statistics: `07_typical_parameters.md`.
- **Flux is not proportional to area.** Opening 0.3 x 0.3 -> 0.5 x 0.5 mm raised IC_B1 from 174 to 300 kHz (x1.72) for an area ratio of x2.78 [S25b:L35,L119-124]: the beam is peaked, so the extra area collects weaker outer beam (inferred). Closing down is therefore cheaper in flux than the area suggests, but costs source-position sensitivity.
- **The best centre moves** with undulator setting and steering (SCU current spread ~0.8 mm horizontal, [L23:L218-278]); it shifts a little between sessions (V centre ~8 um in three weeks, [M26a, B26]); and mechanically the WB-slit table yawed so the IB blade centre moved 0.7 mm in 2026 and needed a further 0.061 mm OB correction by September [T26:L52-56,L871-878; M26b:L160-179]. Always re-peak after steering changes.
- **Calibration method ("just-closed"):** scan one blade across the beam against an ion chamber at high sensitivity; the count rate falls from ~1e4 to the dark floor (e.g. 9930, 8665, 7490, 3955, 1490, 960, 940 for IB -0.125 .. +0.095), and the blade is "just closed" where the counts reach the floor. For a 0.4 mm aperture the blades sit at IB -0.175 / OB +0.305, Bot -0.297 / Top +0.118 (dial values) [T26:L156-205].
- **Beam-defining behaviour:** when the downstream B slits are opened beyond ~0.5 mm they no longer change the image on the tomo camera, because the WB slits are the defining aperture [S25b:L307].

## 2.2 Kohzu slits in B, C and E
- **Roles:** the upstream slit defines the beam, the downstream one cleans up scatter, e.g. C US 0.2 x 0.2 mm (beam-defining) and C DS 0.3 x 0.3 mm (clean-up) [S25a:L202-217]. **Penumbra:** C slits 0.110 mm gave ~0.120 mm at the sample [D22:L178]. **Edge scattering/fringes:** the fringes in the direct beam followed the US-slit vertical size, i.e. the US slit is a source of fringes [M26a:L620-626].
- **Aligning them to the beam** (recipe, [S25b:L145-203], [S25a:L565-592]): open the WB slits (0.5 x 0.5 mm), put the hot spot on a tomo camera, zero the tomo X,Y, then close each Kohzu slit to a few tens of um while watching the tomo image and an ion chamber (IC6-B, IC-E3, IC6-E as the closed-blade indicator), and re-zero so the slit centre equals the hot-spot pixel. Check: "100 um in the slit control = 100 um on the tomo" [S25b:L189-203]; 0.069 mm = 100 px, i.e. 0.69 um/px for GH1 [M26a:L127]. The slit zero depends on the beam position, so it must be redone after every steering change.
- **The beam moves, not the slits:** after beam dumps both C slits had apparently moved +20 um (x) and -10 um (y), and bluesky `check_slits()` found slit centre repeatability at the numerical level (1e-15 mm) [S25a:L279,L433-439]. After the APS-U start the beam moved by 2.5-2.8 mm horizontally and 0.36-0.44 mm vertically at the B slits and ~1.7 mm IB / 0.27 mm up in E [M24:L153-154], [S25a:L190,L592]. Short-term beam motion of 3-15 px on the tomo camera in Jul 2024 was attributed to booster/RF-voltage sag [M24:L248-458].
- **Mechanical failures look like physics:** a B-slit inboard blade stuck "out" (gap larger than commanded) three times in Jun 2025, then hit a hard limit; the mechanics were fine by hand and the motor was moved to another driver channel [S25b:L298-308,L724-727]. A C-DS blade that "did not move" was a driver switched off [M26a:L128,L140,L219]. A C-US slit stage needed a 1/2-inch shim for correct height [M24:L290-292].

## 2.3 Attenuation vs aperture in practice
Closing slits to 0.1-0.2 mm reduces counts ~quadratically for a uniform beam but, because the beam is peaked, less than that near the centre; aperture changes are therefore made with exposures re-scaled (e.g. exposure scaled by 1/6.25 when going 0.2 -> 0.5 mm: 2 s -> 0.32 s [S25a:L233-254]).
